EU Inc. explained: what the proposed “28th regime” could mean for small businesses

Brussels wants founders to be able to set up one company that works across all 27 member states — online, in 48 hours. The proposal is still being negotiated. Here is what is on the table.

By European SME Editorial Team · 7 November 2026 · 4 min read
Key points
  • The European Commission proposed EU Inc., an optional EU-wide company form, on 18 March 2026.
  • It would sit alongside national company forms, and any company could opt in — not only start-ups.
  • The Commission wants Parliament and Council to agree by the end of 2026. Nothing changes for your business yet.

What is proposed

On 18 March 2026 the European Commission published its proposal for EU Inc., an optional, digital-first legal form for companies. It is the cornerstone of what Brussels calls the “28th regime” — a single set of EU-wide rules that works like a 28th legal system next to the 27 national ones.

The headline promises are practical: fully digital incorporation within 48 hours, harmonised templates for founding documents, and access to a common EU scheme for employee stock options, known as EU-ESOs.

Who it is for

Many expected the new form to be reserved for start-ups. It is not. The proposal makes EU Inc. available to any founder who considers it suitable, and existing companies of any size could opt in and stay in the regime.

Some extras are narrower. A simplified, digital winding-up procedure, for example, would apply only to companies that qualify as “innovative start-ups” under a separate Commission recommendation defining innovative start-ups and scale-ups.

One company form for 27 markets is the idea. The detail is still being negotiated.

What it does not do

EU Inc. does not introduce a new EU-wide investment instrument; companies would keep using existing financing tools. The proposal also includes a list of prohibited practices so that EU Inc. companies are treated the same way as national ones, and a central EU registry is planned only for later.

Company law is one piece of the puzzle. Alongside the proposal, the Commission set out a Communication on further initiatives to complete the 28th regime in other policy areas.

Where it stands

The proposal is now with the European Parliament and the Council, and the Commission has asked them to reach agreement by the end of 2026. The European Committee of the Regions and the European Economic and Social Committee both adopted opinions in July 2026, and EU competitiveness ministers have backed the 28th regime — with reservations. A founder-led campaign for the idea was backed by more than 22,000 founders and investors.

What to do now

  • Nothing is required today — EU Inc. does not exist yet.
  • If you plan to set up in other EU countries in 2027 or 2028, follow the negotiations before choosing a structure.
  • If employee share options matter to your hiring, watch how the EU-ESO scheme develops.
  • Ask your legal adviser how an EU Inc. would compare with your current national company form.
