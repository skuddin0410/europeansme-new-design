E-invoicing goes mandatory: where European SMEs must comply in 2026 and 2027

Belgium, Poland and France switched on national e-invoicing mandates this year, and Germany is next. Here is what applies where — and why every cross-border seller will be affected by 2030.

By European SME Editorial Team · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read
Key points
  • Belgium has required B2B e-invoices since 1 January 2026; Poland's KSeF system became mandatory in stages from February 2026.
  • France's mandate began in September 2026 and phases in by company size through 2027. Germany's duty to issue e-invoices starts in 2027.
  • From 1 July 2030, structured e-invoices become mandatory for cross-border B2B sales across the EU.

For years, “e-invoicing” meant emailing a PDF. Not any more. Across the EU, governments are requiring structured electronic invoices — machine-readable files that flow straight from the seller's system to the buyer's, and increasingly to the tax authority too. A PDF sent by email does not count.

The 2026 wave

Belgium moved first this year. Since 1 January 2026, every VAT-registered business must send B2B invoices as structured e-invoices through the Peppol network. The old Hermes platform closed at the end of 2025, so businesses now need a Peppol service provider. Reporting invoice data to the tax authority follows in 2028.

Poland's national system, KSeF, became mandatory for large taxpayers — turnover above PLN 200 million — in February 2026, and for most other businesses in April. Micro-enterprises join in January 2027.

France started its mandate in September 2026 through certified platforms. The obligation to issue e-invoices is phased in by company size through 2027.

Germany: receive now, send soon

Since 1 January 2025, every business in Germany must be able to receive structured e-invoices in the XRechnung or ZUGFeRD formats. The duty to issue them expands from 2027 and applies more broadly from 2028.

A PDF sent by email is not an e-invoice under these rules.

Who is next

Italy has required B2B e-invoicing since 2019, and Romania also has a mandate in force. In all, eight member states now have a domestic B2B mandate, and Ireland, Latvia, Slovakia, Slovenia and Spain have legislated or announced dates. Ireland plans to start in November 2028. The Netherlands has proposed January 2030, with a consultation due in the fourth quarter of 2026.

2030: the EU-wide switch

The EU's VAT in the Digital Age package, adopted in March 2025, sets the end point. From 1 July 2030, structured e-invoicing and digital reporting become mandatory for cross-border B2B transactions within the EU.

Until then, most national mandates apply only to businesses established in that country. A company trading in several member states may face several different rule sets at once — which is why it pays to plan now.

What to do now

  • List the countries where you are established and where you are VAT-registered.
  • Make sure you can already receive structured e-invoices — German customers and suppliers expect it.
  • Choose invoicing software that supports the European standard (EN 16931) and the Peppol network.
  • Agree with your accountant the order in which your markets switch on, and test before each date.
